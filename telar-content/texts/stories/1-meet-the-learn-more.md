The Club regularly commissioned artists for a range of anti-superstitious paraphernalia. Artists illustrated covers for Club reports, skeletons frolicking in the margins, and coffin shape place settings. They also commissioned poems and actors!

This cartoon hails from a collection commissioned by the Club in 1907 to celebrate their trip to Washington D.C. which included cartoons by a variety of artists. The same trip also included "thespian stunts" from "the best playhouses in Washington" among the entertainments for the Club's dinner.

The cartoon depicts a man in a mismatched outfit walking below a ladder only to find himself pelted by bricks. He's further importuned by the date, Friday the 13th, as well as the misfortune of tripping over a cat. In other words: bad luck has befallen him in a maelstrom of superstitious signs. However, far from supporting superstitions as real, the cartoon portrays this man as absurd through his clownish apparel. His appearance is a deliberately far cry from the men in simple, formal black evening attire who composed the usual guests at the Club's dinners. The cartoon pokes fun at his intelligence through his query "Gee, is dis a brain-storm?" further setting the cartoonish man apart from the masculine reason Club members prized.
