title: "What was Spiritualism?"
---

Spiritualism, the belief that the living could communicate with the dead through a sensitive individual--most often a young woman--known as a medium, emerged as a major popular religious movement in the US following a celebrated series of events in upstate New York in 1848.

That year, two young sisters, Kate and Margaret Fox of Hydesville, New York (pictured here), reported mysterious rappings in their home which they claimed represented communications from a spirit. News of the Fox sisters spread rapidly, and within a few years Spiritualism had become a national phenomenon, attracting followers across class lines and generating intense debate in both the popular press and emerging scientific communities.

Women were not merely the primary participants in Spiritualism but its driving force. Mediumship, the central practice of the movement, was understood to require a particular receptivity to spiritual influence that contemporaries associated with femininity, and women dominated the ranks of practicing mediums grasping at religious leadership roles in numbers previously inaccessible. Spiritualism offered something radical: a platform, an audience, and a claim to spiritual authority entirely independent of male institutions and their rules. [Historian Ann Braude's work](https://catabiesman.github.io/13club/sources/) has demonstrated significant connections between spiritualism and progressive political causes, including women's suffrage.  

In other words, when Club members aimed jokes at the expense of Spiritualists and their belief in ghosts, they effectively targeted a movement known for women's leadership.
