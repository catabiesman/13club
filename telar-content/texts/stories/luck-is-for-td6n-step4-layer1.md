Despite establishing "Ladies Nights," no evidence suggests that they permitted women to become members of their gentlemen's Club. However, groups of thirteen ladies around the country founded their own Thirteen Clubs in the following years.  

Notably, the idea of hosting women at their dinners at all appears to have been suggested by ladies themselves in their replies to the Club's survey. For all that the Club's raison d'etre purported to undermine public belief in superstition, their Club activities tended more toward a dining party than any particular educational efforts towards the demographics they imagined as most superstitious.
