---
title: "Troubling \"proof\""
---

#### Death comes for us all
Accounting for member deaths posed an interesting conundrum for the Club, whose evidence that the superstition surrounding 13 diners to a table was unfounded hinged upon participants surviving the year. This ‘proof’ was inevitably threatened by a myriad of uncontrollable factors, ranging from accidents to old age.

Their efforts to document scientific evidence reflects their commitment to intellectualism, but also openly mocked those who, in their view, lacked the wit to see that such evidence was hardly conclusive. Club members regularly invited challenges upon these grounds: "if there is any real danger in the number 13, let us know it. Convince us by intelligent argument supported by proofs, and not by isolated cases of accidents plainly attributable to other causes." When members did, inevitably, die within what they called "the superstition period," they did not investigate this as serious evidence that there might indeed be merit to the danger of 13 but rather doubled down on the logic of coincidences. 

#### Repetition and "good" omens 
The Club delighted in composing lists of "good" events defending 13 and Friday which often celebrated US history or political customs.
The exalted democratic practice of trial by a jury of one's peers redeemed 13: "Every Judge is a chairman of a thirteen club, because the twelve jurors and the judge making thirteen are required to constitute a court for the trial."
The Club merrily observed that "the only man of prominence in history who turned his back upon the original thirteen [colonies] was Benedict Arnold, who died an exile and outcast." In contrast, "the illustrious [George] Washington, entwined with the cabalistic number thirteen, will live forever in the memory of a grateful and reverential people." 

These lists repeatedly sought to 'stick' 13 and Friday to "good" outcomes, rather than dire consequences. By rendering absurd lists of evidence, the Club further both poked fun at the illogic of their superstitious counter-parts.
