---
title: "Troubling \"proof\""
---

Accounting for member deaths posed an interesting conundrum for the Club, whose evidence that the superstition surrounding 13 diners to a table was unfounded hinged upon participants surviving the year. This ‘proof’ was inevitably threatened by a myriad of uncontrollable factors, ranging from accidents to old age.

Their efforts to document scientific evidence reflects their commitment to intellectualism, but also openly mocks those who in their view lacked the wit to see that such evidence was hardly conclusive. Club members regularly invited challenges upon these grounds: "if there is any real danger in the number 13, let us know it. Convince us by intelligent argument supported by proofs, and not by isolated cases of accidents plainly attributable to other causes." The Club used nonsensical evidence lists both in defense of 13's innocence, and in service of their own claims to higher powers of reason as they openly courted absurdity and humor in their evidence collection.
