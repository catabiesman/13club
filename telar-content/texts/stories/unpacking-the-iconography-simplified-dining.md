---
title: "Simplified Dining"
---

This particular menu holds more than a playful engagement with the number thirteen, it also openly mocks then-President (and honorary Club member!) Theodore Roosevelt. President Theodore Roosevelt's infamous campaign to 'simplify' American spelling of English in partnership with the Simplified Spelling Board was met with derision and mockery from the American public.

In 1906, the Club capitalized on this discourse. They printed an excerpt of the revised spelling guide, calling on all reasonable members to adopt it immediately — despite the fact that all their own materials remained composed in the traditional grammar. They doubled down on the joke by devising this "Simplified Dining" menu, which featured thirteen courses ranging from "Just Oysters" and "Plain Soup" to "French Peas, simplified by American raising" and "Black Coffee, milk omitted for simplification."
