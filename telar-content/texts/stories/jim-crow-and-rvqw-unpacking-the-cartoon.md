---
title: "unpacking the cartoon"
---

This cartoon appeared in a 1907 Club report celebrating a Club trip to Washington D.C. Club rhetoric and cartoons ridiculed Black men for bowing to superstition, and thus as lacking the intellectual capacity for self-governance. Often, this involved describing or drawing the person in question as child-like. 

In a very real sense, the Club imagined superstitious people as relics of the past at odds with modernity--a modernity they imagined themselves to occupy.  

The text of the cartoon further points toward the political stakes of Black freedom and voting rights. The text "under ten years free" operates as a double-entendre, both a reference to transit fare and a reference to the short time in which Black folks had (at least on paper) gained citizenship. The cartoon only loosely invokes superstition through luck--despite the fact that the Club commissioned illustrators to ridicule superstition. The fact that the Club regarded a joke targeting blackness as equivalent to targeting superstition further affirms that by 1907, Club humor had effectively 'stuck' these two concepts together.
