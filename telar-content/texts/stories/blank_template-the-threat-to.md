---
title: "The threat to the Republic"
---

>"The only way to have brave, honest, intelligent, conscientious public men, men without superstition, is to do what we can to make the average citizen brave,  conscientious and intelligent. If you wish to see courage in the presidential chair, conscience upon the bench, intelligence of the highest order in Congress; if you expect public men to be great enough to reflect honor upon the Republic, private citizens must have the courage and the intelligence to elect, and to sustain, such men."

Col. Robert Green Ingersoll warned of the dangers of superstitious beliefs to the American system of governance in a 1886 speech to the Club. His words demonstrate that while members mocked and satirized superstition, they understood the stakes of belief to be quite serious.
