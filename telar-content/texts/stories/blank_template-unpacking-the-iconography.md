---
title: "unpacking the iconography"
---

**The Members**: Thirteen men in evening dress appear at the dining table. The men did indeed dine in formal attire, a detail which added hilarity through sharp contrast to their silly antics. 

You'll also notice that they're drinking alcohol! Members set themselves apart from the growing influence of the women's temperance by including alcohol in their dinners, often giving thirteen tongue-in-cheek toasts. indeed, in 1883, they have a toast to Ladies, calling them "Always superstitious and always dear." By ironically raising an alcoholic toast to women--a demographic they imagined as both naturally more vulnerable to superstition and likely allies of the temperance movement--the Club affirmed their claims to rationalism and manhood. 

**The Grim Reaper**: This playful cameo from death himself is typical in Club materials. They often featured a grinning grim reaper joining in Club festivities, or even raising a toast with members. Notice also that the door he enters through features thirteen in roman numerals. 

The Club seized on every opportunity to invoke the number thirteen, and through that repetition render it absurd rather than menacing. For example, becoming a member cost $1.13 and fees thereafter consisted of 13¢. They held their dinners on the 13th of the month (also the deadline for payment of member dues), and began their dinners at 13 minutes after the hour. Their menus consistently featured 13 courses, and 13 alcoholic options to choose from. 

By playfully invoking death--both literally through iconography and by-proxy through superstitions alleged to cause death--members sought to render superstition as an object of humor rather than fear. 

**The Motto**: The Club translated their Latin motto, "Morituri te salutamus," to "we who are about to die salute you." This irreverent salvo is in keeping with their ironic sense of humor, seen above in their provocative toasts! 

The owl is also a notable choice, as the owl in Western culture historically symbolizes wisdom and intelligence--but also carries associations with the occult! 

Later versions of their Club symbol would feature ladders in a nod to the superstition against walking underneath ladders, but retained the owl wings.
