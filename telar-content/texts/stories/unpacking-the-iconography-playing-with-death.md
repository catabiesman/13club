---
title: "Playing with Death"
---

The _New York Times_ regularly reported death imagery in the Club's illustrations, physical decorations, and absurdist rituals. For example, a 1894 article observed that "The dinner tickets bore the imprint of skulls and skeletons, and had other cheerful devices, such as "Odes to Death." the wine list was printed on black cards, shaped like coffins, and artistically decorated with crossbones." A 1904 article noted that the Club offered skulls as souvenirs -- whether these were fake or real is unclear. 

At the annual banquet of 1907, the Club elaborately laid superstition itself to rest in a funeral of sorts: "under dimmed lights old superstition was buried with solemn ceremony, a huge black coffin, properly inscribed, being carried through the banquet hall on the shoulders of thirteen members of the club" reported the _Times_. 

By playfully invoking death--both literally through iconography and by-proxy through superstitions alleged to cause death--members sought to render superstition as an object of humor rather than fear.
