---
title: "Tellers of tales"
---

Club humor suggested that women not only believed in but even originated many popular superstitions through telling tales to the children in their care. 

A Club speech observed that for most children, “some superstitious feeling” was “imbibed with his mother's milk.” While the Club reminisced fondly about the "harmless" superstitions of childhood, they asserted that “age and intelligent reason lift the veil of delusion” as the child matured into a man. Any adult who persisted in superstitious belief, by this logic, failed to complete the developmental journey from childhood fantasy to masculine rationality. He remained, embarrassingly, stranded in mental infancy or effeminacy, unsuited to the demands of citizenship. Adult women, by nature of their gender, could never mature into this kind of intellectualism within the logic of the Club, but remained attached to childish beliefs through Club humor. This state of affairs did not concern the Club in earlier years as women had little political influence, and Congress had only recently dismissed suffragists efforts to include women in the voting rights granted by the 15th amendment.
