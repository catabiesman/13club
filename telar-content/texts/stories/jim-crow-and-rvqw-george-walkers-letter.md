---
title: "George Walker's letter of protest"
---

The _New York Tribune_ article goes on to include excerepts of a letter of protest from Black comedian George W. Walker, though the paper noted that the letter was not read out to the diners at the Club gathering. 
Walker inquired "how it came to pass that your learned society failed to invite a representative of my race to speak at your dinner." "Is it possible," he wondered, "that you have members who are seeking to emancipate themselves from superstition and yet they fail to be broad enough to ask a man of African blood to be present and to part in your deliberations?" 

Walker's letter highlights the hypocrisy of the Club for failing to live up to its own stated ideals of intellectual inclusion. His words exposed the contradiction of "learned" men, with supposedly "fair-minded and progressive" values as blatantly racist in their Club practices.

This critique was in keeping with Walker's successful vaudeville career, where he often played an upstanding 'dandy' without any blackface makeup. His performances and his letter to the Club alike refuted prevailing popular depictions of Black folks on minstrel stages during this period.
